Will GameNative run on my phone?

Short answer

It depends on the game and on your device. Across 1,319 devices, 107,518 of 137,997 game-and-device combinations with enough data (78%) are rated Great or Works by the app; 21,484 are Unreliable and 8,995 are Broken. By GPU family, the share of combinations rated Great or Works is Qualcomm Adreno 81% of 109,196; ARM Mali 64% of 22,896; Samsung Xclipse 69% of 3,729; Imagination PowerVR 67% of 1,945; Other 41% of 224. Of 6,310,370 play sessions in the 180 days up to November 27, 2026, 5,063,913 (80%) were playable.

These are games people chose to try, and the most-played ones are mostly lighter or older games, so these percentages overstate what to expect from demanding new releases.

A session counts as playable when the game ran for more than 60 seconds at 15 FPS or better. That is a low bar: playable does not mean smooth. Find your device or GPU below for its own numbers, including the popular games that don't run reliably on it. How these numbers work

Finding your phone

  • Search the device list by name or model number (for example "samsung SC-52F"). Model variants are merged into one entry.
  • Don't see your device? Devices with fewer than 5 games with enough data are not listed. Look up which GPU your phone has (it is part of the chipset named in your phone's specifications) and check the GPU table.
  • Some phone models are sold in variants with different GPUs. Device pages say so when units of one model reported different GPUs.
  • For user-submitted reports on a specific game, with notes and settings, search the Compatibility page. Those are separate from the play-session data used here.

How these numbers work

  • The data is play-session telemetry from the GameNative app: the same rollup behind the compatibility badge shown in the app. It covers 6,310,370 sessions in the 180 days up to November 27, 2026, on 1,319 devices and 80 GPUs, across 39,805 games.
  • A session counts as playable when the game ran for more than 60 seconds at 15 FPS or better. That is a low bar: playable does not mean smooth.
  • Each game on each device or GPU gets the app's verdict once it has at least 5 sessions there: Broken when under 10% of sessions were playable, Unreliable when under 50%, Great when at least 70% were playable and the median FPS is 25 or more, otherwise Works. Recent sessions, which use a stricter check, count double.
  • A verdict can be worse than the playable share suggests: the app lowers it (never raises it) when stricter recent sessions, data from the same GPU family, or user ratings disagree, for example when fewer than 15% of the devices that rated a game ever gave it 4 or 5 stars or played it for 15+ minutes.
  • Each device or GPU gets a summary from the share of its games rated Great or Works: Most tested games run at 75% or more, Mixed from 50%, and Most tested games do not run reliably below that.
  • These are games people chose to try, and the most-played ones are mostly lighter or older games, so these percentages overstate what to expect from demanding new releases.
  • Model variants are merged into one device entry; each device page lists the model numbers it covers. Devices and GPUs need at least 20 games with a verdict for their own page, and at least 5 to be listed here.
  • The Compatibility page lists user-submitted reports instead, with notes and settings. It is a separate source.
  • Snapshot generated November 27, 2026.
